## [1] The Science and Practice of Trend-Following Systems
**Link** : https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19497
**Strategy fit** : A (TSMOM)

### 1. Edge identifié
The paper's claim is structural, not a new empirical signal: trend-following is profitable exactly
when volatility-normalized returns carry positive long-run autocorrelation, which in the frequency
domain means excess spectral mass at low frequencies — a Poisson-kernel reading of the return
spectrum. It further shows trend-following's well-known positive-skew payoff is partly a *structural*
artifact of the filter itself (it appears even under pure white noise, peaking near half the filter
span), not necessarily evidence of a genuine autocorrelation edge. It derives closed-form Sharpe ratios
under ARFIMA return processes and a cost-optimal lookback span balancing signal capture against
turnover cost. Mechanism: persistent regime-driven drift (or long-memory autocorrelation) in
volatility-normalized returns, captured by a moving-window filter, monetized net of the turnover cost
that filter length implies.

### 2. Évidence empirique
- Univers testé : analytical/Monte Carlo (ARFIMA, white-noise processes); empirical section evaluates
  systems on a set of "liquid contracts" (asset class/period not detailed in the abstract, but framed
  as futures-style liquid instruments, not crypto).
- Métriques : closed-form Sharpe ratio (function of autocorrelation, drift, kurtosis loading); no
  single headline empirical Sharpe/t-stat given in the abstract.
- Méthodologie validation : primarily analytical derivation confirmed by Monte Carlo; the empirical
  section (truncated in the fetched abstract) reports that European/American/TSMOM variants are
  strongly cross-correlated across the tested contracts — no walk-forward/OOS split described.
- Robustness : addressed analytically (via ARFIMA long-memory and frequency-domain arguments) rather
  than through empirical regime/cost-stress testing.

### 3. Applicabilité à Algo_claude
- **Sujet adjacent ou directement applicable ?** Directly on Strategy A's asset class (TSMOM), but as
  theory/methodology rather than a portable new signal.
- **Coût d'implémentation** : LOW (no new data or infra — reading and reference only).
- **Conflit stratégies existantes ?** Non — doesn't propose modifying Strategy A; Strategy A is FROZEN
  (since 2026-05-30) so no live change is in scope regardless.
- **Data requis** : aucune (papier théorique).
- **Compatibilité propfirm** : n/a.
- **Match avec archived_strategies.yaml ?** Fichier absent — vérifié contre `research_history.md` :
  "Multi-asset TSMOM: Sharpe 0.21 corrected" est le seul entry TSMOM archivé, et ne recoupe pas ce
  cadre ARFIMA/spectral — pas de match, pas de dégradation de verdict requise.

### 4. Honest skeptic check
1. The result "trend-following works iff autocorrelation is positive" is close to tautological — it
   doesn't establish that Strategy A's specific 3-asset (BTC/ETH/SOL) 28-day-window signal currently
   sits in a regime with genuine positive autocorrelation; the paper offers a lens, not a verdict.
2. The structural-skewness finding cuts against over-crediting TF Sharpe/skew as edge evidence — it's
   as much a caution about self-deception in trend-following performance analysis as it is a
   validation of the strategy class.
3. The empirical section evaluates "liquid contracts," almost certainly traditional futures (equity
   index / bond / commodity), not 4h/daily crypto; the cost-optimal-span result may not transfer to
   crypto's very different vol and liquidity regime without independent verification.

### 5. Décision proposée
**REFERENCE_ONLY.** Strategy A is FROZEN; this is useful theoretical background for a future TSMOM v4
redesign (particularly the cost-optimal span derivation and the caution about structural skew) but
proposes no new testable hypothesis today, and modifying a frozen strategy is out of scope for this
routine.

## [2] When Alpha Disappears: A One-Switch Benchmark for Decision-Time Leakage in Financial Backtests
**Link** : https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23959
**Strategy fit** : Méthodologie

### 1. Edge identifié
Not a trading edge — a diagnostic benchmark. It isolates, one evaluation-protocol convention at a time,
which choices around a nominally clean "predict then trade at next open" setup actually leak
future information and inflate apparent performance. Mechanism: certain conventions (same-day-open
execution combined with information from later in that day's bar; temporal features centered using
information not truly available at decision time) look innocuous but quietly violate the T-1/T signal
discipline the benchmark is built to expose, while other conventions tested (global feature
normalization, future-informed graph structure) turned out to be comparatively weak leakage sources.

### 2. Évidence empirique
- Univers testé : two daily-OHLCV equity panels, six model families, yearly tests 2016-2024.
- Métriques : reports which protocol toggles produce "large and stable" inflation in predictive and
  trading metrics versus which are "weak in most settings" — framed as a diagnostic ranking, not a
  Sharpe/return claim.
- Méthodologie validation : walk-forward-style yearly splits with one evaluation convention toggled at
  a time, holding the data panel, model family, horizon, portfolio rule, and cost convention fixed.
- Robustness : explicitly scoped as diagnostic rather than a tradable-alpha claim — no cost/slippage
  stress test needed since there's no strategy being defended.

### 3. Applicabilité à Algo_claude
- **Sujet adjacent ou directement applicable ?** Directly applicable as an audit lens, not as a
  strategy — reinforces the project's own signal-at-T-1/trade-at-T hard rule with a concrete taxonomy
  of subtler ways that rule gets silently violated.
- **Coût d'implémentation** : LOW — conceptual; could inform a self-audit pass over existing backtest
  code (e.g. checking whether "next-open execution" silently allows same-bar information, or whether
  rolling/centered features leak forward).
- **Conflit stratégies existantes ?** Non — audit lens, not a new signal.
- **Data requis** : aucune.
- **Compatibilité propfirm** : n/a.
- **Match avec archived_strategies.yaml ?** Fichier absent — `research_history.md` n'a pas d'entrée de
  ce type (benchmark de leakage) ; le plus proche est la famille de leçons L26/L59 (ambiguïté
  same-bar, re-pricing stop-entry/bracket-exit), issue de la pratique du repo plutôt que d'un benchmark
  formel — complémentaire, pas un doublon.

### 4. Honest skeptic check
1. Tested only on daily-OHLCV equities with six generic ML model families — none of Algo_claude's
   actual signal shapes (vol-targeted tanh CS signal, ORB entry, funding-rate carry) are represented,
   so the *magnitudes* of inflation reported don't transfer, only the conceptual checklist does.
2. As a benchmark paper it makes no Sharpe/edge claim to interrogate — the risk is the opposite one:
   over-applying its specific taxonomy to a context (e.g. Strategy B's 8h-rebalance carry data) it
   never tested, and concluding false confidence either way.
3. "Diagnostic rather than a claim of tradable alpha" also means it can't be scored against this
   project's own AMTP/walk-forward gates — its value is entirely as a code-review lens, not a testable
   hypothesis, so it can't "graduate" through the normal pipeline the way a signal candidate would.

### 5. Décision proposée
**REFERENCE_ONLY.** Genuinely useful as an audit checklist for existing/future backtest code
(especially ahead of any Strategy A/C redesign), but it isn't a strategy candidate and doesn't fit the
TEST_NOW alpha-hunt pipeline schema (no `strategy_class`, no signal to specify).

## [3] Conformal Kelly: Conformal Prediction Intervals as the Scale in Fractional Kelly Position Sizing
**Link** : https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01494
**Strategy fit** : Méthodologie / Propfirm

### 1. Edge identifié
Proposes scaling a fractional-Kelly position size inversely to the width of a 75%-coverage conformal
prediction interval around a return forecast (narrower band → larger position, wider band → smaller
position), combined with a rule that cuts leverage when realized downside misses exceed the interval's
nominal miss rate. The mechanism is risk-managed position sizing conditioned on forecast uncertainty,
not a new source of directional alpha — the underlying return forecast/asset universe generating the
base signal is not described in the fetched abstract.

### 2. Évidence empirique
- Univers testé : six-year "development window" (2016-2021), apparently an equity/S&P-relative
  portfolio, under trading costs and strict leverage caps.
- Métriques : 28.5%/yr annualized net log growth, Sharpe 1.34, MaxDD 27.7%, vs. 15.9%/yr for S&P 500
  buy-and-hold and 21-22%/yr for passive portfolios at matched leverage; the downside-cut rule reduces
  MaxDD to 20.3% while raising Sharpe, beating "all 40 placebo timings" (rank p = 1/41).
- Méthodologie validation : states results came from an autonomous LLM-agent search over ~200 sizing
  configurations on the development window, with data from 2022 onward "sealed" and the winning
  configuration pre-registered before the holdout test — but the holdout-period result itself is not
  given in the truncated abstract.
- Robustness : a 40-placebo-timing test is reported for the downside-leverage-cut component only, not
  for the primary 200-configuration sizing search.

### 3. Applicabilité à Algo_claude
- **Sujet adjacent ou directement applicable ?** Directly on Méthodologie/Propfirm — fractional-Kelly
  sizing under drawdown constraints is an explicit project focus area.
- **Coût d'implémentation** : MED — would require reproducing the conformal-interval machinery and
  supplying a credible base forecast, neither of which is specified in the paper.
- **Conflit stratégies existantes ?** Non.
- **Data requis** : indéterminé — le modèle de prévision sous-jacent générant les intervalles n'est
  pas décrit.
- **Compatibilité propfirm** : **INCOMPATIBLE** as reported — MaxDD 27.7% already exceeds this
  project's own PROPFIRM_PASS gate (-10%) and its RESEARCH_PASS gate (-25%), before any
  Algo_claude-specific re-verification.
- **Match avec archived_strategies.yaml ?** Fichier absent — pas de correspondance dans
  `research_history.md` (aucune entrée Kelly/conformal antérieure).

### 4. Honest skeptic check
1. The paper self-discloses an autonomous LLM-agent search over ~200 configurations on the *same*
   development window used to report the headline 28.5%/Sharpe-1.34 numbers — a textbook
   multiple-testing setup, and no DSR/AMTP-style correction is applied to that primary search (only the
   smaller 40-placebo test on the downside-cut sub-component gets a placebo p-value).
2. The reported MaxDD (27.7%) already fails this project's own RESEARCH_PASS bar at face value, before
   considering real-world execution slippage or that the underlying forecast model isn't specified.
3. Single author, no described institutional data/compute affiliation; the "sealed 2022+ holdout"
   claim can't be verified from the abstract alone, and the paper frames its central finding (slow,
   unweighted rolling quantiles beat adaptive intervals) as running counter to the conformal-prediction
   literature's usual advice — plausible as a genuine contrarian result, but at least as consistent with
   picking, out of 200 tried configurations, the one that happened to look best in-sample.

### 5. Décision proposée
**REJECT.** The paper's own reported MaxDD (27.7%) already exceeds this project's RESEARCH_PASS gate
(-25%), and the disclosed 200-configuration autonomous search is a significant, self-admitted
overfitting risk with no multiple-testing correction applied to the primary result — not a "revisit
later" case, a "doesn't clear this project's own bar as reported" case.
