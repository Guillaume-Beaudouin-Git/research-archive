## Summary
Portfolio selection under exponential, power, and logarithmic utility is solved explicitly under multivariate affine Volterra (rough volatility) dynamics with portfolio constraints. Under rough volatility (H<0.5), the optimal portfolio is more conservative than the classical Merton solution and exhibits mean-reversion of optimal allocation over intermediate horizons. No-short-selling constraints materially reduce welfare relative to unconstrained portfolios.

## Why it might be useful
The rough volatility + portfolio constraints framing is theoretically adjacent to Strategy A's vol targeting (long-only, VOL_TARGET_LIVE=0.18) and the propfirm constraints (no-short-selling for Propr, drawdown caps). The finding that rough volatility induces more conservative optimal allocation corroborates the current conservative sizing approach. However, this paper derives theoretical optimal policies, not a tradeable signal or implementable procedure beyond what current strategy code already does with rolling vol targeting.
