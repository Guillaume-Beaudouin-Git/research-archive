## Summary
An asset pricing study of Japanese zaibatsu (conglomerate) stocks during wartime economic controls (1930-1943). Using a four-portfolio sort (zaibatsu × military orientation) and a CAPM-AR(p)-SV event-study framework, the paper finds that zaibatsu affiliation explains abnormal returns during wartime controls, with delayed adjustment to group-level shocks. The finding is interpreted as "institutionally contingent efficiency": markets continued to respond to public information but also priced private access to credit, materials, and procurement — suggesting informational advantages from political connections under wartime controls.
