## Summary
This paper studies multiperiod competitive groundwater markets where a hydrological stock evolves stochastically. It establishes existence and structure of competitive equilibria, characterizes how user heterogeneity, stock externalities, and pumping costs shape equilibrium prices and quantities, and shows that common-pool extraction inefficiencies (tragedy of the commons) persist even in competitive equilibria. The social planner's optimal regulatory instruments are also characterized. The paper is a contribution to environmental and resource economics rather than to financial markets.
