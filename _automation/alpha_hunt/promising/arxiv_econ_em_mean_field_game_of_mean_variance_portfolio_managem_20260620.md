## Summary
A mean-field game formulates portfolio choice where each agent's risk aversion switches depending on whether their wealth is above or below the population average, creating a new class of time-inconsistent mean-field games. Nash equilibrium is characterized via forward-backward SDE techniques with closed-form solutions under specific regimes. The model captures herd-like behavior emerging from benchmark-relative preferences.

## Why it might be useful
This is primarily theoretical machinery with no direct path to a testable signal. The peer-reference risk aversion is a partial theoretical explanation for L11 (beta dominance in long-only crypto CS): if all agents benchmark against BTC, they collectively amplify momentum regardless of IC. The time-inconsistency structure is thematically related to the propfirm sizing problem (RL for time-inconsistent control, another item in this batch), but both are too theoretical for the current research roadmap.
