## Summary
A theoretical framework analyzing how leverage interacts with manipulation in event-linked prediction markets. The paper distinguishes market-price manipulation (affects quoted prices) from real-world outcome manipulation (affects the underlying event), and shows that leverage affects these asymmetrically: it scales market-price manipulation linearly but shifts the cost-benefit threshold for real-outcome manipulation. Informed trading rents under leverage scale three ways: direct multiplication, Sharpe-ratio preservation, and detection-cost amortization. The regulatory section analyzes three manipulation channels introduced by dynamic margin engines and halt protocols.
