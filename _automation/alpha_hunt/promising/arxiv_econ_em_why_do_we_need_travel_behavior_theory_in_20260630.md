## Summary
This discussion paper argues that AI/ML models in travel demand research improve predictive accuracy at the cost of behavioral grounding. It proposes goal pursuit theory (GPT) as a complement to random utility models, with GPT explicitly modeling how agents activate context-dependent goals (hedonic, gain, normative), resolve conflicts between competing objectives, and make sequential decisions. The paper demonstrates three transport applications — activity scheduling, vehicle ownership, and location choice — using matrix factorization to capture latent goal interactions.
