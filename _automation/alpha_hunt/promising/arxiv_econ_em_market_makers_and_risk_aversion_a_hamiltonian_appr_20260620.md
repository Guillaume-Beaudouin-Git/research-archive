## Summary
Price and inventory are modeled as anharmonic oscillators with nonlinear coupling, where market makers' risk appetite is the key parameter controlling the degree of chaotic behavior. The paper demonstrates that unpredictable price changes can arise purely from the nonlinear market-maker interaction, without external shocks or noise. This challenges the view that all price randomness reflects external information arrival.

## Why it might be useful
The nonlinear coupling framework provides a theoretical explanation for the oscillatory price behavior that Strategy B's delta-neutral carry exploits. The risk-appetite parameter maps loosely to the inventory risk component in the Avellaneda-Stoikov model (cited in the 1202 run's bid-ask paper). Background theory for execution modeling; no direct test path without fitting the Hamiltonian model to HL data.
