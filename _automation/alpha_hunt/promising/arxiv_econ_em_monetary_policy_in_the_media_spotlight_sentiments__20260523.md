## Summary
This Canadian paper constructs monetary policy sentiment indicators from over 50,000 newspaper articles using three methods: standard dictionary classification, transformer-based models, and a generative-AI framework. Media sentiment is embedded into a behavioral New-Keynesian model where the central bank reacts to sentiment and sentiment follows an explicit law of motion. Key empirical findings are that media sentiment shifts household inflation expectations, improves out-of-sample GDP and inflation forecasts, and loads positively on the Bank of Canada's estimated Taylor rule when treated as endogenous to the policy process. A Bayesian SVAR identifies a narrative shock that accounts for a non-trivial share of medium-horizon macroeconomic variance.
