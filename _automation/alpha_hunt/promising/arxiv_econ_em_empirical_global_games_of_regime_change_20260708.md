## Summary
The paper builds a structural econometric model of coups d'état using global-games theory, where the coordination problem among coup plotters is resolved via an equilibrium-selection device rather than left implicit as in reduced-form coup literature. It separates the "feasibility" of a regime overthrow (how weak the incumbent regime actually is) from its "desirability" (perceived payoff to rebelling), and shows both can be recovered from observable country covariates under exclusion restrictions. Estimation uses simulated maximum likelihood combined with a contraction-mapping fixed-point solver, standard machinery for discrete-choice games with multiple equilibria. The authors demonstrate counterfactual exercises varying regime strength, coup payoffs, and information quality about the incumbent.
