## Summary
A critique of entrenched statistical practice in clinical/medical research — over-reliance on hypothesis tests to compare treatments, prediction-accuracy methods disconnected from actual patient care, poor meta-analysis practice, and pervasive overconfidence — arguing the dysfunction is structurally held in place by weak statistical training, researchers' dependence on gatekeeping biostatisticians, and the incentive structures of journals, funders, and regulators.
